# 1. Basic Concepts of Ratio

## What is Ratio?

Ratio means comparison of two quantities of the same kind.

Example:

20 : 40 = 1 : 2

So, 20 is to 40 as 1 is to 2.

### Ratio can be written as

- a : b
- a/b
- a to b

All represent the same ratio.

### Example

2 : 5 = 2/5 = 2 to 5

---

## Important Terms

For:

a : b

- a → Antecedent / First term
- b → Consequent / Second term

---

## ⚠️ Same Unit Rule

Before finding a ratio, units must be the same.

Example:

2 kg : 500 g

Convert kg into grams:

2 kg = 2000 g

Therefore:

2000 : 500
= 4 : 1

### ⭐ Short Trick

> Ratio → First check units → Then simplify.

---

# 2. Properties of Ratio

## Property 1 — Multiplication

If both terms are multiplied by the same number, ratio remains unchanged.

a : b = ka : kb

Example:

3 : 4

Multiply by 2:

6 : 8

Both ratios are equivalent.

---

## Property 2 — Division

Both terms can be divided by the same non-zero number.

Example:

24 : 36

HCF = 12

24 ÷ 12 : 36 ÷ 12

= 2 : 3

### ⭐ Short Trick

> Ratio simplify → Divide both terms by HCF.

---

# 3. Proportion

When two ratios are equal, they are said to be in proportion.

a : b :: c : d

or

a/b = c/d

### Example

2 : 3 :: 4 : 6

Because:

2/3 = 4/6

---

## Most Important Formula

a : b :: c : d

Cross multiply:

a × d = b × c

Therefore:

ad = bc

### ⭐ Short Trick

> Proportion → Cross multiply.

---

# 4. Properties of Proportion

## 4.1 Means and Extremes

For:

a : b :: c : d

- a and d → Extremes
- b and c → Means

Therefore:

Product of Extremes = Product of Means

ad = bc

---

## 4.2 Mean Proportional

If:

a : b :: b : c

then b is the mean proportional.

Formula:

b = √(ac)

### Example

Find mean proportional between 5 and 45.

√(5 × 45)

= √225

= 15

### ⭐ Trick

Mean proportional:

√(First × Third)

---

## 4.3 Third Proportional

If:

a : b :: b : c

then c is the third proportional.

Formula:

c = b²/a

### ⭐ Trick

Third proportional:

Second² ÷ First

---

# 5. Types of Ratio

Suppose:

a : b = 8 : 1

---

## 5.1 Duplicate Ratio

Square both terms.

a : b → a² : b²

Example:

8 : 1

= 64 : 1

### ⭐ Trick

Duplicate = Square

---

## 5.2 Sub-Duplicate Ratio

Take square root of both terms.

a : b → √a : √b

Example:

8 : 1

= √8 : √1

= 2√2 : 1

### ⭐ Trick

Sub-Duplicate = Square Root

---

## 5.3 Triplicate Ratio

Cube both terms.

a : b → a³ : b³

Example:

8 : 1

= 512 : 1

### ⭐ Trick

Triplicate = Cube

---

## 5.4 Sub-Triplicate Ratio

Take cube root.

a : b → ∛a : ∛b

### ⭐ Trick

Sub-Triplicate = Cube Root

---

## 5.5 Inverse Ratio

Reverse the ratio.

a : b → b : a

Example:

3 : 4

Inverse = 4 : 3

---

## Multiple-Term Inverse Ratio

For:

a : b : c

Inverse:

1/a : 1/b : 1/c

Then multiply by LCM to remove fractions.

### Example

3 : 4 : 6

Inverse:

1/3 : 1/4 : 1/6

LCM = 12

Multiply by 12:

4 : 3 : 2

### ⭐ Trick

> Inverse → Take reciprocal → LCM.

---

# 6. Combining Two or More Ratios

Suppose:

A : B = 2 : 3

B : C = 5 : 6

We need:

A : B : C

The common term is B.

Make B equal.

LCM of 3 and 5 = 15

First ratio:

2 : 3
×5
= 10 : 15

Second ratio:

5 : 6
×3
= 15 : 18

Therefore:

A : B : C = 10 : 15 : 18

### ⭐ Short Trick

> Common term ko same karo.

---

## General Method

If:

A : B = a : b

B : C = c : d

Then:

1. Find LCM of b and c.
2. Make both B terms equal.
3. Combine the ratios.

# 7. Variation

There are two important types:

1. Direct Variation
2. Inverse Variation

---

# 7.1 Direct Variation

If:

A increases → B increases

A decreases → B decreases

Then A varies directly with B.

Notation:

A ∝ B

Therefore:

A = kB

So:

A/B = constant

---

## Formula

A₁/B₁ = A₂/B₂

### Example

A varies directly with B.

When:

B = 6
A = 12

Find A when B = 12.

12/6 = A/12

2 = A/12

A = 24

### ⭐ Short Trick

Direct → Divide

A/B = Constant

### Memory Trick

Direct = Same Direction

↑ ↑

↓ ↓

---

# 7.2 Inverse Variation

If:

A increases → B decreases

A decreases → B increases

Then A varies inversely with B.

Notation:

A ∝ 1/B

Therefore:

AB = constant

---

## Formula

A₁B₁ = A₂B₂

### Example

Y is inversely proportional to X.

X₁ = 5
Y₁ = 0.4

X₂ = 4

Therefore:

5 × 0.4 = 4 × Y₂

2 = 4Y₂

Y₂ = 0.5

### ⭐ Short Trick

Inverse → Multiply

AB = Constant

### Memory Trick

Inverse = Opposite Direction

↑ ↓

↓ ↑

---

# 8. Invertendo, Alternendo, Componendo & Dividendo

Suppose:

a/b = c/d

---

## 8.1 Invertendo

Reverse both ratios.

a/b = c/d

Therefore:

b/a = d/c

### Trick

Invertendo = Invert

---

## 8.2 Alternendo

Interchange the middle terms.

a/b = c/d

Therefore:

a/c = b/d

### Trick

Alternendo = Alternate / Exchange

---

## 8.3 Componendo

Add numerator and denominator.

a/b = c/d

Therefore:

(a + b)/b = (c + d)/d

---

## 8.4 Dividendo

Subtract denominator from numerator.

a/b = c/d

Therefore:

(a - b)/b = (c - d)/d

---

## ⭐ Easy Memory Trick

INVERTENDO
→ उल्टा

ALTERNENDO
→ Middle terms exchange

COMPONENDO
→ Plus (+)

DIVIDENDO
→ Minus (-)

---

# 9. Addendo Property

If:

a/b = c/d = e/f

Then:

(a + c + e)/(b + d + f)

will have the same ratio.

### Example

2/3 = 6/9 = 8/12

Therefore:

(2 + 6 + 8)/(3 + 9 + 12)

= 16/24

= 2/3

### ⭐ Short Trick

> Equal ratios → Numerators add, denominators add.

---

# 10. Equivalent Ratio Property

If:

a/b = c/d

Then equivalent ratios can be formed by combining corresponding terms.

Important forms:

(a + c)/(b + d) = a/b

and

(a - c)/(b - d) = a/b

provided the expressions are defined.

### ⭐ Trick

> Same ratio + corresponding terms → Equivalent ratio.

---

# 11. Division of a Quantity in Given Ratio

Suppose X is divided in:

m : n

Total parts:

m + n

---

## First Part

X × m/(m+n)

## Second Part

X × n/(m+n)

---

## Example

Divide 153 in ratio:

4 : 5

Total parts:

4 + 5 = 9

One part:

153/9 = 17

First share:

4 × 17 = 68

Second share:

5 × 17 = 85

Answer:

68 : 85

---

## ⭐ Super Trick

For X divided in m:n:

Total Parts = m+n

One Part = X/(m+n)

Shares:

First = m × One Part

Second = n × One Part

---

# 12. Bringing Numbers into Proportion

Typical question:

What should be added to four numbers so that they become proportional?

Suppose:

a, b, c, d

After adding x:

(a+x)/(b+x) = (c+x)/(d+x)

Cross multiply:

(a+x)(d+x) = (b+x)(c+x)

Then solve for x.

---

## Shortcut Formula — Addition

For:

(a+x)/(b+x) = (c+x)/(d+x)

x = (bc - ad)/(a + d - b - c)

---

## For Subtraction

If:

(a-x)/(b-x) = (c-x)/(d-x)

Then:

x = (ad - bc)/(a + d - b - c)

---

## ⭐ Exam Trick

Instead of trying to guess the new numbers:

1. Form the new ratio.
2. Cross multiply.
3. Solve x.

---

# 13. Converting One Ratio into Another

Suppose:

a : b

has to be converted into:

c : d

by adding x.

Then:

(a+x)/(b+x) = c/d

Cross multiply and solve.

---

## Example

Convert:

9 : 16

into:

2 : 3

by adding x.

Set:

(9+x)/(16+x) = 2/3

Cross multiply:

3(9+x) = 2(16+x)

27 + 3x = 32 + 2x

x = 5

Answer:

5

---

## If x is Subtracted

Use:

(a-x)/(b-x) = c/d

Then cross multiply.

### ⭐ Short Trick

Added:

(a+x)/(b+x)

Subtracted:

(a-x)/(b-x)

Then:

Cross Multiply

---

# 14. Ratio from Sum & Difference

Suppose:

(A+B) : (A-B) = m : n

Then:

A : B = (m+n) : (m-n)

---

## Example

Sum : Difference = 9 : 2

Then:

A : B

= (9+2) : (9-2)

= 11 : 7

### ⭐ Super Shortcut

Sum : Difference = m:n

Numbers:

(m+n) : (m-n)

---

# 15. Finding A:C from A:B and B:C

Suppose:

A : B = a : b

B : C = c : d

We need:

A : C

---

## Method

Make the common term B equal.

### Example

A:B = 3:4

B:C = 6:7

LCM of 4 and 6 = 12

First:

3:4 × 3

= 9:12

Second:

6:7 × 2

= 12:14

Therefore:

A:B:C = 9:12:14

So:

A:C = 9:14

---

## ⭐ Direct Shortcut

If:

A:B = a:b

B:C = c:d

Then:

A:C = ac : bd

Then simplify if required.

### Example

A:B = 3:4

B:C = 6:7

A:C:

(3×6) : (4×7)

= 18:28

= 9:14

---

# 16. Finding A:B:C from mA = nB = pC

Very important shortcut.

Given:

mA = nB = pC

Then:

A = k/m

B = k/n

C = k/p

Therefore:

A:B:C

= 1/m : 1/n : 1/p

Then multiply by LCM to remove fractions.

---

## Example

3A = 5B = 6C

Therefore:

A:B:C

= 1/3 : 1/5 : 1/6

LCM(3,5,6) = 30

Multiply by 30:

10 : 6 : 5

Therefore:

A:B:C = 10:6:5

---

## ⭐ Super Shortcut

mA = nB = pC

↓

Take reciprocal of coefficients

↓

A:B:C = 1/m : 1/n : 1/p

↓

Multiply by LCM

---

## 🧠 Memory Trick

Coefficient ulta → Ratio

3A = 5B = 6C

3, 5, 6

↓

1/3 : 1/5 : 1/6

---

# 17. Ratio of Linear Expressions

Suppose:

x : y = a : b

Find:

(mx + ny) : (px + qy)

---

## ⭐ Shortcut

Take:

x = a

y = b

Because only the ratio of x and y matters.

Then directly substitute.

---

## Example

Given:

x:y = 2:3

Find:

(3x+2y) : (2x+5y)

Take:

x = 2

y = 3

First expression:

3x + 2y

= 3(2) + 2(3)

= 6 + 6

= 12

Second expression:

2x + 5y

= 2(2) + 5(3)

= 4 + 15

= 19

Therefore:

12 : 19

---

## ⭐ Super Trick

Whenever:

x:y = a:b

and question contains x and y:

> Take x=a and y=b.

No need to find actual values.

---

# 18. Comparing Two Ratios

Suppose:

5 : 12

and

3 : 8

Which is greater?

Write as fractions:

5/12 and 3/8

Cross multiply:

5 × 8 = 40

3 × 12 = 36

Since:

40 > 36

Therefore:

5/12 > 3/8

So:

5:12 is greater.

---

## ⭐ Super Shortcut

For:

a:b

and

c:d

Compare:

a × d

and

b × c

If:

ad > bc

then:

a:b > c:d

If:

ad < bc

then:

a:b < c:d
